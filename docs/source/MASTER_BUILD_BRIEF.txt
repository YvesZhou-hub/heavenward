4. RULE FIDELITY AND EDGE CASES
==================================================

Create an executable rules contract, not just prose documentation.

Preserve the exact important distinctions:

Starting deck:
5 Strike, 5 Defense, and 2 selected Dao Arts.
Two selection rounds; five eligible choices per round.

Card zones:
Draw, Hand, Discard, Exhaust.
Draw five at Player Turn start; hand maximum ten.
Overflow goes to Discard rather than disappearing.

Discard:
Explicit player/enemy discard is Active Discard.
Natural end-turn discard and overflow are not.

Grades:
Ding, Bing, Yi, Jia, Huang, Xuan, Di, Tian.
Normal realm grade is not an absolute cap.
One grade above the current normal grade is usable.
Human Realm therefore includes access to Xuan through eligible
above-realm upgrades at Spirit Transformation.

Do not count grade variants as distinct card designs.

Divine Abilities:
Drawn and paid for as cards, persistent after activation,
ungraded, not upgradeable, sellable, and acquired only through
eligible Heavenly Tribulations.
Maximum four Human Realm slots.
No Merchant, Event, normal-combat, or Elite acquisition loopholes.

Damage:
Correctly distinguish attack modifiers, external damage,
summon-owned offensive stats, Damage Link, and Direct HP Loss.
Do not model overlapping damage properties as mutually exclusive
categories when that would break the source rules.

Preserve FLOOR rounding, individual multi-hit resolution,
Armor Pierce consumption on actual HP damage,
Protective Qi reduction/decay, and all specified stack caps.

Tidal Domain:
Not a summon. No summon slot, HP, Intercept, sacrifice,
summon buffs, or anti-summon consumption.
Preserve threshold timing, state selection, Momentum changes,
and the unused-Dao-Yuan condition.

Flying Swords:
Combat-only Token attacks; cost zero, base damage two,
Exhaust after use, no permanent sale or upgrade.
No arbitrary per-turn generation limit.

Retain / Formation:
Count turns actually retained across the specified turn boundary,
not time spent in hand or number of draws.

Re-Refinement:
Temporary grade changes revert correctly after combat.

Preserve every listed cross-Path synergy.
Strong combinations are allowed when they follow the rules.

10. FIRST-CLASS GAME UX
==================================================

Design around decisions, not around showing every variable at once.

Players should quickly understand:
whose turn it is, available Dao Yuan, enemy Intent,
playable cards, valid targets, expected consequences,
and the next required action.

Support both click-to-play and drag-to-target where appropriate.
Provide clear cancel behavior and keyboard alternatives.
Do not make precision dragging mandatory.

Enemy Intent must remain readable during effects.
Do not silently change a committed intent after the player acts.

Show meaningful target previews:
Armor impact, HP impact, multi-hit behavior,
relevant reductions, and self-harm where applicable.

Use the same resolver or a verified non-mutating simulation path.
Previewing must not consume RNG or change state.
Do not expose hidden outcomes, draws, or uncommitted randomness.
Represent uncertain outcomes honestly.

Use contextual choices rather than unnecessary modal interruptions.
Tidal state selection must preserve the source rule while fitting
smoothly into the end-turn flow.

Inspection panels should preserve combat context.
Unknown Draw-Pile order must remain hidden unless revealed by an effect.

Deckbuilding UX must include:
current deck inspection during rewards,
upgrade before/after comparison,
sale consequences, grade restrictions,
Path/keyword filtering, and clear skip/replacement behavior.

Protect irreversible actions without placing confirmation dialogs
on every ordinary card play.

New players need progressive onboarding.
Experienced players need speed and minimal repetition.
Tutorials must teach the actual game rather than a disconnected demo.
